# Research watch

## Scope

Find new research leads for human and clinician review.

Do not diagnose, rank treatments, estimate personal benefit, or tell the person what to do.

## Before every run

1. Ask for the exact condition, treatment, biomarker, and decision question.
2. Draft the search terms.
3. Remove names, full dates, record numbers, clinics, and locations.
4. Show the search terms to the person.
5. Explain which external search services will receive them.
6. Wait for consent.

Rare combinations can identify someone. Say so when the query is unusually specific.

Never upload the health profile or raw records to a search service.

## Source order

1. Regulators and current clinical guidelines.
2. Clinical-trial registries.
3. PubMed and the journal's primary paper.
4. Conference records and preprints, clearly labeled.

Open every source before reporting it. Check the title, authors, date, population, and actual result. Check for corrections, retractions, and trial-status changes.

- Main result:
- Limits:
- Conflicts of interest:
- Why it was flagged for review:
- Question for the clinician:

Quote little. Link the source.

## Hard rules

- Open every link before treating it as a source.
- Verification requires the original source.
- A preprint is preliminary.
- A clinician or trial team confirms eligibility.
- Expect gaps in every research watch.
- Clinical relevance belongs to the treating clinician.
- Urgent symptoms go to a clinician or local emergency service now.
